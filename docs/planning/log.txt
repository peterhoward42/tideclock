This is about a client server data flow for a web app. The web app displays the
times of the high and low tide today, at a location you have chosen - on a 12
hour clock face. It follows that the clock has conceptually two modes - it is
either showing the time period from 00h00 to noon today, or from noon to
midnight today. The fundamental data is obtained via an external webservice.
When you make a request to that service you get a set of times for your chosen
location that go from 00h00 today, for an interval of about 3 days. The data you
get includes the date and time (UTC) for each high and low tide event, and so is
completely self defining. The current web app architecture holds some tide data
locally that has been fetched from the external tide service, and it caches it,
both in memory and persisted. There are several events that can occur that mean
that new tide data must be fetched.

It turns out that the caching mental model has been very complicated and I think
fragile to implement, and I suspect the root cause is the fundamental modelling
of the problem. What follows is what I believe to be simpler and more aligned
model to think about it, that I hope will reveal a simpler architecture.

The new model still depends on the external tide time service mentioned. That
part is unchanged. The new model is predicated on a new conceptual DRY entry
point for the data flow as follows. 

The entry point is the clock needs the information right now for the high and
low tides times today for the 12 hour daytime or nighttime slot of today as
appropriate as implied my local time including daylight saving time. I.e. to
match what the user expects the times to mean on the clock face it displays. The
app will have a persistent store of the most recent data it has fetched from the
tide service. This store is likely the browsers local storage. I propose that
this is not a cache, and that the persisted data is the only client-side source
of truth. It follows that the conceptual entry point can inspect the client side
store to see if it contains the high and low tide events that fall into the
required 12 hour window and is for the requested location. It will fetch new
data if the required data is missing and use that to completely replace the
client side store. Only then will it yield the requested information to the
caller.

You will note that I havent' said anything about the UX or App-side various
reasons for wanting the data right now. I contend that it doesn't matter to help
us judge the merit of this architecture for simplicity.
